Cp vs Cpk: what your capability numbers really say

LeanPro Academy knowledge library

Cp and Cpk both compare the spread of a process with its specification limits, but they answer different questions.

Cp asks whether the process spread would fit inside the limits if it were perfectly centred. It is the tolerance width divided by six standard deviations.

Cpk asks whether it actually fits, given where the process mean sits today. It uses the distance from the mean to the nearest limit, divided by three standard deviations.

If Cp is good but Cpk is poor, the process is capable but off-centre, and adjusting the mean is usually the quickest fix. If both are poor, the variation itself must come down, which needs root cause work on materials, methods or machines.

A common guideline is Cpk of at least 1.33 for an established process. Always check that the process is stable on a control chart before calculating capability, and that the measurement system is trustworthy.
