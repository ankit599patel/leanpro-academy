Writing a problem statement that leads somewhere

LeanPro Academy knowledge library

A good problem statement describes the gap between what should happen and what is happening, with facts. It does not contain a cause or a solution.

Use simple questions: what is the problem, where was it seen, when did it start, how big is it and how do we know? “Cap torque rejections on Line 2 rose from 0.5% to 2.1% between 3 and 10 November, measured at final inspection” is a problem statement. “Operators are careless with capping” is a guess about the cause.

Keep it narrow. A statement that covers every defect in the plant cannot be solved in one project. Pick the largest piece, usually found with a Pareto chart.

Check it with the team at the gemba. If people who do the work do not recognise the problem as written, rewrite it.

A clear statement makes root cause analysis faster, because everyone looks at the same facts instead of defending their own idea.
