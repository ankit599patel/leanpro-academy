365 Days of Lean · Days 301–330

Leadership

Free daily lessons, days 301–330.

Day 301 · Leadership & Daily Management

Leader Standard Work

The routine that keeps leaders at the gemba.

Leader standard work is a defined routine of tasks for leaders at each level, such as board reviews, gemba walks, audits and coaching, with times and frequencies, so the management system runs consistently.

Key takeaway: Leaders need standard work too.

Day 302 · Leadership & Daily Management

Daily Management System

A daily rhythm to see, solve and sustain.

A daily management system links visual boards, short tiered meetings, leader standard work and problem-solving so that performance gaps are seen and acted on every day.

Key takeaway: See it daily, solve it daily.

Day 303 · Leadership & Daily Management

Tier Meetings

Short meetings that escalate issues fast.

Tier meetings are short, standing meetings held at successive levels, from team to department to plant, each reviewing performance and escalating issues that need help from the next level.

Key takeaway: Short, standing, on time, with escalation.

Day 304 · Leadership & Daily Management

Visual KPI Board

Show performance where the work is done.

A visual KPI board displays a few key measures with targets, trends and status colours at the workplace, so the team can see performance and discuss actions.

Key takeaway: Few measures, clear status, linked actions.

Day 305 · Leadership & Daily Management

SQDCP

Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost, People.

SQDCP is a common structure for daily boards and meetings that reviews Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost and People in that order, keeping attention on what matters most.

Key takeaway: Start every review with safety.

Day 306 · Leadership & Daily Management

Escalation Process

Know who to call, and when.

An escalation process defines when a problem should be raised to the next level, how, and how quickly the next level must respond, so problems do not stay hidden or stuck.

Key takeaway: Escalation rules turn waiting into action.

Day 307 · Leadership & Daily Management

Gemba Leadership

Lead from where the work happens.

Gemba leadership means leaders spend regular time at the workplace, observing processes, asking questions, supporting problem-solving and removing obstacles, rather than managing only from reports.

Key takeaway: Ask, listen, help, follow up.

Day 308 · Leadership & Daily Management

Coaching at Gemba

Teach by asking, at the place of work.

Coaching at gemba helps people improve their own problem-solving by asking open questions about the target, the current condition, obstacles and the next step, instead of giving answers.

Key takeaway: Ask the next question instead of giving the answer.

Day 309 · Leadership & Daily Management

Hoshin Kanri Intro

Align everyone to a few breakthrough goals.

Hoshin Kanri is a strategy deployment method that selects a few breakthrough objectives, translates them into goals and actions at each level, and reviews progress regularly.

Key takeaway: Few priorities, deployed to every level.

Day 310 · Leadership & Daily Management

Policy Deployment

Connect strategy to daily work.

Policy deployment cascades goals and measures from the top down, with plans agreed through dialogue (catchball) between levels, so each team knows how its work supports the strategy.

Key takeaway: Agree goals through dialogue, not decree.

Day 311 · Leadership & Daily Management

KPI vs KAI

Measure results and the activities that drive them.

A KPI (key performance indicator) measures results, such as OEE or OTIF. A KAI (key activity indicator) measures the improvement activities that drive those results, such as SMED events or audits completed.

Key takeaway: Manage the activities to move the results.

Day 312 · Leadership & Daily Management

Leading vs Lagging Indicators

Predict the result versus report the result.

Leading indicators are measures that change before the outcome and can be influenced now, such as PM compliance. Lagging indicators show the outcome after the fact, such as breakdown hours.

Key takeaway: Lead with what you can influence today.

Day 313 · Leadership & Daily Management

Accountability vs Blame

Own the process; do not blame the person.

Accountability means owning commitments and outcomes and following through. Blame focuses on finding someone at fault. A blame culture hides problems; an accountable culture surfaces and fixes them.

Key takeaway: Ask 'what failed?' before 'who failed?'.

Day 314 · Leadership & Daily Management

Team Problem Solving

Solve problems together across roles.

Team problem solving uses the knowledge of operators, technicians, quality and supervisors together, with a simple structured method, to find causes and agree countermeasures.

Key takeaway: The people closest to the problem solve it best.

Day 315 · Leadership & Daily Management

Cross-Functional Alignment

Different functions, one goal.

Cross-functional alignment means production, maintenance, quality, planning and supply chain agree shared goals, priorities and handoffs, so their actions support the whole value stream.

Key takeaway: Shared goals beat department goals.

Day 316 · Leadership & Daily Management

Skill Matrix

Who can do what, and who needs training.

A skill matrix lists team members against the skills and tasks of the area, showing each person's level from 'in training' to 'can train others', to plan training and coverage.

Key takeaway: Plan coverage before someone is absent.

Day 317 · Leadership & Daily Management

Training Within Industry

A proven method to train, improve and lead.

Training Within Industry (TWI) is a set of programs: Job Instruction (how to train), Job Methods (how to improve jobs) and Job Relations (how to lead people). Job Instruction uses four steps: prepare, present, try out, follow up.

Key takeaway: If the learner has not learned, the instructor has not taught.

Day 318 · Leadership & Daily Management

Standardized Handover

Pass work over the same way every time.

A standardized handover uses a fixed format to pass on information between people or functions, such as status, issues, safety points and open actions, so nothing important is lost.

Key takeaway: Same format, every handover.

Day 319 · Leadership & Daily Management

Shift Handover

Start the next shift fully informed.

A shift handover passes key information from the outgoing to the incoming shift: safety issues, quality concerns, equipment status, production status and open actions, ideally face to face at the line board.

Key takeaway: Face to face, at the board, with a checklist.

Day 320 · Leadership & Daily Management

Meeting Waste

Meetings that waste time instead of creating value.

Meeting waste includes meetings without a purpose, agenda or decisions, the wrong people present, late starts and overruns, and actions that are never followed up.

Key takeaway: Purpose, agenda, decisions, actions, on time.

Day 321 · Leadership & Daily Management

Decision Rights

Be clear about who decides what.

Decision rights define who makes which decisions, who must be consulted and who must be informed, so decisions are made quickly at the right level.

Key takeaway: Decide at the lowest level with the right information.

Day 322 · Leadership & Daily Management

Management by Fact

Decide with data, not with opinions.

Management by fact means basing decisions on reliable data and direct observation rather than assumptions, while being clear about the quality and limits of the data.

Key takeaway: In God we trust; all others bring data.

Day 323 · Leadership & Daily Management

Psychological Safety

People can speak up without fear.

Psychological safety is a shared belief that team members can raise problems, ask questions, admit mistakes and suggest ideas without fear of embarrassment or punishment.

Key takeaway: Thank people for raising problems.

Day 324 · Leadership & Daily Management

Recognition for Kaizen

Recognize improvement to get more of it.

Recognition for kaizen acknowledges people's improvement ideas and efforts, publicly and promptly, which encourages continued participation. Recognition can be simple and does not need to be costly.

Key takeaway: Recognize quickly, publicly and sincerely.

Day 325 · Leadership & Daily Management

Change Management

Help people move from the old way to the new.

Change management is a structured approach to help people adopt a new way of working: explain why, involve those affected, plan the transition, train, support and reinforce until the change sticks.

Key takeaway: Explain why, involve early, support through.

Day 326 · Leadership & Daily Management

Resistance to Change

Resistance is information, not an enemy.

Resistance to change is a natural response when people feel uncertain, lose control or see risks. Listening to concerns, involving people and showing early results helps reduce it.

Key takeaway: Listen to the resistance; it often points to a real risk.

Day 327 · Leadership & Daily Management

Leadership Case Study

An illustrative leadership change story.

A leadership case study shows how changes in leader routines and behaviour affected team performance. This example is illustrative; use your own observations and data in real cases.

Key takeaway: Change the routine to change the culture.

Day 328 · Leadership & Daily Management

Daily Review Routine

A fixed daily routine to review performance.

A daily review routine is a set sequence at a fixed time: review yesterday's results against targets, discuss today's risks, agree actions and owners, and check previous actions.

Key takeaway: Same time, same place, same agenda.

Day 329 · Leadership & Daily Management

Lean Culture

Shared habits of improvement and respect.

Lean culture is a workplace where people see problems as opportunities, follow and improve standards, go to the gemba, respect each other and improve every day as normal work.

Key takeaway: Culture is what people do every day.

Day 330 · Leadership & Daily Management

Leadership Review

Routines, people and alignment.

Leadership review: build leader standard work and tiered meetings, use visual KPIs, escalate quickly, coach at the gemba, deploy strategy to every level, and create a culture where people speak up.

Key takeaway: Lead with routines, coaching and respect.
