5S & Workplace Organisation

Free daily lessons, days 31–60.

Day 31 · 5S & Visual Management

What Is 5S?

Five steps to a safe, clean and organized workplace.

5S is a method for organizing the workplace: Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize and Sustain. It removes clutter, gives everything a place, and makes abnormal conditions easy to see.

Key takeaway: A place for everything, and everything in its place.

Day 32 · 5S & Visual Management

Sort (Seiri)

Keep only what is needed for the work.

Sort (Seiri) means separating needed items from unneeded ones and removing what is not required for current work. It frees space and reduces searching and safety risks.

Key takeaway: When in doubt, red-tag it.

Day 33 · 5S & Visual Management

Set in Order (Seiton)

A clear, labelled place for every needed item.

Set in Order (Seiton) means arranging needed items so they are easy to find, use and return. Location is based on frequency of use, with labels, outlines and shadow boards.

Key takeaway: Anyone should find any item in 30 seconds.

Day 34 · 5S & Visual Management

Shine (Seiso)

Clean to inspect, and inspect to find problems.

Shine (Seiso) means cleaning the work area and equipment regularly, and using cleaning as inspection. Leaks, loose bolts and wear are found while cleaning.

Key takeaway: Cleaning is inspection.

Day 35 · 5S & Visual Management

Standardize (Seiketsu)

Make the first three S a written, visual routine.

Standardize (Seiketsu) means creating simple standards, such as checklists, photos and visual rules, so that Sort, Set in Order and Shine are done the same way by everyone, every time.

Key takeaway: Make the right condition obvious to everyone.

Day 36 · 5S & Visual Management

Sustain (Shitsuke)

Turn 5S into a habit that lasts.

Sustain (Shitsuke) means building the discipline to follow the 5S standards every day through audits, leader support, training and recognition.

Key takeaway: Without Sustain, 5S slowly disappears.

Day 37 · 5S & Visual Management

Red Tagging

A simple tag to decide what stays and what goes.

Red tagging marks items whose need is unclear during Sort. Tagged items go to a holding area for a fixed period, then are kept, relocated or disposed of by an agreed owner.

Key takeaway: Tag it, hold it, decide it.

Day 38 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Audit

A regular check that 5S standards are being met.

A 5S audit uses a simple scored checklist to assess each S in a zone. The score shows trends and highlights gaps; the purpose is improvement, not blame.

Key takeaway: Audit to improve, not to blame.

Day 39 · 5S & Visual Management

Shadow Boards

Every tool has a home you can see.

A shadow board shows the outline of each tool in its fixed place. A missing tool is visible at a glance, which saves searching and protects against loss.

Key takeaway: An empty outline is an instant alarm.

Day 40 · 5S & Visual Management

Visual Controls

Signals that show the normal condition at a glance.

Visual controls are signs, colours, markings and indicators that show the correct condition, so anyone can see immediately whether things are normal or not.

Key takeaway: If it needs explaining, it is not yet visual.

Day 41 · 5S & Visual Management

Floor Markings

Lines on the floor that organize space and flow.

Floor markings define walkways, work zones, storage locations and safety areas using agreed colours and shapes. They guide movement and make out-of-place items obvious.

Key takeaway: Agree the colour code, then apply it everywhere.

Day 42 · 5S & Visual Management

Labeling & Identification

Clear labels so the right item is always used.

Labeling and identification means marking materials, locations, equipment and status clearly so that people use the right item and avoid mix-ups.

Key takeaway: Unlabelled items invite mistakes.

Day 43 · 5S & Visual Management

Point-of-Use Storage

Store items where they are used.

Point-of-use storage keeps tools, parts and materials at the place where they are used, in the quantity needed. It reduces walking, searching and handling.

Key takeaway: Shorten the reach, shorten the time.

Day 44 · 5S & Visual Management

Before/After 5S

Show the change with the same view, before and after.

A before/after 5S comparison uses photos taken from the same position to show the change in an area. It makes progress visible and helps sustain the new standard.

Key takeaway: Same angle, same place: let the photos speak.

Day 45 · 5S & Visual Management

5S in Stores

Organized stores mean faster, error-free picking.

5S in stores organizes racks, bins and locations so materials are easy to find, count and issue in the right order, with clear labels and min/max levels.

Key takeaway: If it cannot be found, it does not exist.

Day 46 · 5S & Visual Management

5S in Maintenance

Find tools and parts fast when equipment stops.

5S in maintenance organizes tools, spares and workbenches so technicians can find what they need quickly during breakdowns and planned jobs.

Key takeaway: Faster repairs start with an organized workshop.

Day 47 · 5S & Visual Management

5S in Production

A clean line runs better and safer.

5S in production keeps lines organized, with clear material locations, clean machines and visible standards, so abnormalities are seen quickly and changeovers are faster.

Key takeaway: Organize the line, and the line runs smoother.

Day 48 · 5S & Visual Management

5S in Quality Lab

Order in the lab protects results.

5S in the quality lab organizes glassware, reagents, samples and instruments so tests are done correctly and on time, with clear labels and expiry control.

Key takeaway: An organized lab gives reliable, faster results.

Day 49 · 5S & Visual Management

Visual Management

See the status of work without asking.

Visual management makes the status of work, standards and problems visible to everyone at the place of work, so teams can see at a glance whether they are on track.

Key takeaway: Make it visible, then make it better.

Day 50 · 5S & Visual Management

Andon

A signal that calls for help immediately.

Andon is a visual and sometimes audible signal used to show that a process needs help, such as a light, a board or a cord. It allows problems to be raised and responded to fast.

Key takeaway: Raise the problem fast; respond faster.

Day 51 · 5S & Visual Management

Status Boards

Boards that show where the work stands.

Status boards display key information about work in progress, such as orders, machine status or job progress, so that the team shares one picture of the situation.

Key takeaway: One board, one shared picture.

Day 52 · 5S & Visual Management

Abnormality Visibility

Design the workplace so problems cannot hide.

Abnormality visibility means designing standards and visuals so that any deviation from normal stands out immediately, prompting a quick response.

Key takeaway: Normal should look normal; abnormal should shout.

Day 53 · 5S & Visual Management

Color Coding

One agreed colour language across the site.

Colour coding uses consistent colours to show meaning, such as status, material type or zone. It only works if the code is agreed, documented and used the same way everywhere.

Key takeaway: Same colour, same meaning, everywhere.

Day 54 · 5S & Visual Management

One-Point Lessons

One idea, one page, explained in minutes.

A one-point lesson (OPL) is a short visual training sheet that explains a single topic, such as a check or a method, in a few minutes at the workplace.

Key takeaway: Teach one point at a time, at the place of work.

Day 55 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Common Mistakes

Why many 5S programs fade after the first push.

Common 5S mistakes include treating it as a one-time clean-up, skipping Standardize and Sustain, no ownership of zones, and audits used to blame rather than improve.

Key takeaway: 5S is a habit, not an event.

Day 56 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Sustainment

Keep 5S alive month after month.

5S sustainment relies on clear owners, short daily routines, regular audits, leaders who check and support, and recognition of teams that keep improving.

Key takeaway: Owners, routines and leaders keep 5S alive.

Day 57 · 5S & Visual Management

Visual Factory

A factory that tells its own status.

A visual factory uses signs, boards, markings and signals so that anyone walking through can understand what is happening, what is normal and where help is needed.

Key takeaway: Walk through and understand without asking.

Day 58 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Case Study

How a team brought order to a packing area.

A 5S case study follows one area through all five steps, showing the starting condition, the actions taken and how the new standard is sustained. Use measured data from your own site when you tell the story.

Key takeaway: Tell the story with photos and your own measured results.

Day 59 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Checklist

A quick daily check for any area.

A 5S checklist turns the standard into simple yes/no checks that any team member can complete in a few minutes, so gaps are found and fixed daily.

Key takeaway: Five checks, five minutes, every shift.

Day 60 · 5S & Visual Management

5S Review

Sort, Set, Shine, Standardize, Sustain, and make it visual.

This month covered 5S and visual management: organize the workplace, make the normal condition obvious, signal problems quickly, and build the habits that keep it that way.

Key takeaway: Organize it, make it visible, keep it that way.
