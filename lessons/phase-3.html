Day 61 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

What Is Kaizen?

Change for the better, a little every day.

Kaizen means continuous improvement through small, practical changes made by the people who do the work. Many small improvements, standardized and repeated, add up over time.

Key takeaway: Small changes, made every day, by everyone.

Day 62 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Kaizen vs Innovation

Small steady steps versus big leaps.

Kaizen improves through many small, low-cost steps by everyone. Innovation makes larger jumps through new technology or investment. Organizations need both, and kaizen keeps gains from slipping between leaps.

Key takeaway: Use innovation to leap; use kaizen to climb and hold.

Day 63 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Small Improvements

The power of many small fixes.

Small improvements are quick, low-risk changes that remove a daily irritation or waste. Because they are easy to try, more people take part and more problems get solved.

Key takeaway: Do not wait for the big project. Fix what you can today.

Day 64 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

PDCA Cycle

Plan, Do, Check, Act: the learning loop.

PDCA is a four-step cycle for improvement: Plan a change based on facts, Do it on a small scale, Check the result against the plan, and Act by standardizing or adjusting. Then repeat.

Key takeaway: Plan small, test fast, learn, standardize.

Day 65 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

PDSA Cycle

Same loop, with a focus on study and learning.

PDSA (Plan, Do, Study, Act) is Deming's version of the improvement cycle. 'Study' stresses comparing results with predictions and learning why, not only checking whether a target was met.

Key takeaway: Predict, test, then study what you learned.

Day 66 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

A3 Problem Solving

One page that tells the whole problem story.

A3 problem solving uses a single A3-size sheet to follow a structured path: background, current condition, goal, root cause analysis, countermeasures, plan and follow-up. It keeps thinking clear and shareable.

Key takeaway: If it does not fit on one page, the thinking is not clear yet.

Day 67 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Problem Statement

Describe the problem with facts, not solutions.

A problem statement describes what is wrong, where, when and how much, compared with the standard or expectation. It does not include causes or solutions.

Key takeaway: State the gap. Leave the solution for later.

Day 68 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Define the Gap

Current condition versus the desired condition.

Defining the gap means measuring the current condition, stating the desired condition, and describing the difference clearly so the team knows exactly what it is trying to close.

Key takeaway: No clear gap, no clear improvement.

Day 69 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Containment vs Correction

Stop the bleeding, then cure the disease.

Containment is a quick action to protect the customer from a problem, such as holding or sorting suspect stock. Correction (and corrective action) removes the cause so the problem does not return.

Key takeaway: Contain first, but never stop at containment.

Day 70 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Corrective vs Preventive Action

Fix what happened, and prevent what could happen.

Corrective action removes the cause of a problem that has occurred, to prevent recurrence. Preventive action removes the cause of a potential problem before it happens.

Key takeaway: Corrective looks back; preventive looks ahead.

Day 71 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Root Cause Thinking

Look past the symptom to what really caused it.

Root cause thinking means asking what condition allowed a problem to happen, and verifying it with evidence, instead of reacting only to the visible symptom.

Key takeaway: Treat causes, not symptoms.

Day 72 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

5 Whys

Ask why until you reach a cause you can act on.

5 Whys is a simple method of asking 'why?' repeatedly, each answer based on facts, until a root cause is found that can be fixed. The number five is a guide, not a rule.

Key takeaway: Each 'why' must be backed by evidence.

Day 73 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Fishbone Diagram

Organize possible causes before jumping to answers.

A fishbone (Ishikawa or cause-and-effect) diagram groups possible causes of a problem into categories such as Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement and Environment, so the team considers all angles.

Key takeaway: List causes widely, then verify the few that matter.

Day 74 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Pareto Analysis

Find the vital few causes that matter most.

Pareto analysis ranks problems or causes by frequency or impact so the team can focus on the largest contributors first. It is based on the observation that a few causes often account for most of the effect.

Key takeaway: Fix the biggest bars first.

Day 75 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Brainstorming Rules

Rules that bring out more and better ideas.

Good brainstorming sets clear rules: one topic, quantity before quality, no criticism during idea generation, build on others' ideas, and everyone gets a turn. Evaluation comes afterwards.

Key takeaway: Generate first, judge later.

Day 76 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Affinity Diagram

Group many ideas into a few clear themes.

An affinity diagram organizes a large number of ideas or observations into natural groups. The team sorts notes silently, then names each group, revealing the main themes.

Key takeaway: Sort the noise into a few themes.

Day 77 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Problem-Solving Team

The right people, roles and time to solve it.

A problem-solving team brings together people who know the process, the problem and the data, with a clear leader, a sponsor who removes barriers, and agreed meeting time.

Key takeaway: Include the people closest to the problem.

Day 78 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Kaizen Event

A focused burst of improvement in a few days.

A kaizen event is a short, focused improvement workshop, typically lasting a few days, where a cross-functional team analyses one process, tests changes on the spot and standardizes the result.

Key takeaway: Prepare well, act fast, follow up.

Day 79 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Suggestion System

A simple way for everyone to share ideas.

A suggestion system collects, reviews and implements ideas from employees. Good systems respond quickly, let people implement their own ideas where possible, and recognize contributions.

Key takeaway: Fast responses keep ideas coming.

Day 80 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Standardize the Improvement

Lock in the gain so it does not slip back.

Standardizing an improvement means updating the work instruction, training everyone affected, adding visual aids and checks, so the new method becomes the normal way of working.

Key takeaway: An improvement is not done until it is the standard.

Day 81 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Verify Results

Prove the improvement with before/after facts.

Verifying results means comparing measured performance before and after a change, over a long enough period, to confirm the improvement is real and the problem has not moved elsewhere.

Key takeaway: No data, no claim.

Day 82 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Horizontal Deployment

Spread a good fix to every similar place.

Horizontal deployment (yokoten) shares a proven improvement with other lines, shifts or sites that have similar processes, so the same problem is prevented everywhere.

Key takeaway: Fix it once, apply it everywhere it fits.

Day 83 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Kaizen Newspaper

A simple board to track improvement actions.

A kaizen newspaper is a visual list of problems and actions, showing each issue, its owner, due date and status. It keeps small improvements moving and visible.

Key takeaway: Every action gets an owner and a date.

Day 84 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Kaizen Metrics

Measure the activity and the results of kaizen.

Kaizen metrics track both effort (such as ideas submitted, implemented and people involved) and outcomes (such as safety, quality, delivery and cost improvements linked to the changes).

Key takeaway: Measure participation and results together.

Day 85 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Common RCA Mistakes

Traps that lead to the wrong cause.

Common root cause analysis mistakes include stopping at the first answer, blaming people, relying on opinion instead of data, and choosing a solution before the cause is verified.

Key takeaway: 'Human error' is a starting point, not a root cause.

Day 86 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Problem-Solving at Gemba

Solve problems where they happen.

Problem-solving at gemba means going to the actual place, observing the process and the failed part, and talking to the people involved before analyzing and deciding.

Key takeaway: Hold the part, watch the process, ask the operator.

Kaizen Case Study

An illustrative kaizen at a labelling station.

A kaizen case study shows the problem, the small changes tried and how the result was verified. This example is illustrative; replace it with measured results from your own site.

Key takeaway: Show the before, the change and the verified after.

Day 89 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

PDCA in Daily Work

Use PDCA in everyday decisions, not just projects.

PDCA in daily work means treating each small change as a test: plan it, try it, check what happened at the end of the shift, and decide whether to keep, adjust or drop it.

Key takeaway: Every day is a chance to run one small PDCA.

Day 90 · Kaizen & Problem Solving

Problem Solving Review

Define, analyze, fix, verify and spread.

This month covered kaizen and problem solving: state the problem with facts, contain it, find and verify the root cause, apply countermeasures with PDCA, standardize and deploy horizontally.

Key takeaway: Facts, root cause, test, standardize, spread.
