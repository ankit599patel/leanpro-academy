Day 121 · Inventory Optimization

ABC & XYZ Analysis

Classify items by value and by demand pattern.

ABC analysis ranks items by annual value: A items are few but high value, C items are many but low value. XYZ analysis ranks items by demand variability: X is steady, Z is irregular. Combining them sets the right control method for each item.

Key takeaway: Different items deserve different controls.

Day 122 · Cost of Poor Quality

Prevention & Appraisal Cost

Spend on prevention to save on failure.

Prevention costs include training, process design and maintenance that stop defects from happening. Appraisal costs include inspection, testing and audits that find defects. Shifting money from appraisal to prevention usually lowers total quality cost.

Key takeaway: Prevention is cheaper than detection.

Day 123 · Cost of Poor Quality

Internal & External Failure Cost

Failures cost most when they reach the customer.

Internal failure costs, such as scrap and rework, occur before delivery. External failure costs, such as complaints, returns and lost business, occur after delivery and are usually much larger.

Key takeaway: Catch problems inside, prevent them at source.

Day 124 · Cost of Poor Quality

COPQ Case

Show what poor quality really costs.

A cost of poor quality case lists internal failure, external failure, appraisal and prevention costs for one product line. Illustrative case for learning; replace with your own measured data.

Key takeaway: Make the hidden costs visible.

Day 125 · Cost of Poor Quality

Hidden Cost & Profit Improvement Strategy

Much quality cost never appears in accounts.

Hidden costs include extra inspection, expediting, overtime, lost capacity and damaged reputation. Profit improves when these are made visible, measured and attacked with problem solving.

Key takeaway: Make the hidden cost visible.

Day 126 · Cost of Poor Quality

Defect Classification

Not all defects are equally serious.

Defect classification groups defects by severity, commonly critical (safety or legal risk), major (affects function or customer use) and minor (cosmetic). It guides sampling, reaction and priority.

Key takeaway: Match the reaction to the severity.

Day 127 · TPM Integration

8 Pillars

The pillars that hold up TPM.

TPM is often described with pillars: autonomous maintenance, planned maintenance, focused improvement, quality maintenance, early equipment management, training, safety and environment, and TPM in offices, built on a 5S foundation.

Key takeaway: Build on 5S, then raise the pillars.

Day 128 · TPM Integration

Autonomous Maintenance

Operators care for their own equipment.

Autonomous maintenance gives operators responsibility for basic equipment care: cleaning, inspection, lubrication, tightening and early detection of abnormalities, following clear standards.

Key takeaway: Operators are the first line of equipment care.

Day 129 · TPM Integration

Planned Maintenance

Maintenance scheduled before failure.

Planned maintenance is maintenance work scheduled in advance, based on time, usage or condition, to prevent failures and keep equipment in good condition.

Key takeaway: Plan the stop before the machine plans it for you.

Day 130 · TPM Integration

Breakdown Analysis

Run to failure, then repair.

Breakdown maintenance means repairing equipment after it fails. It can be acceptable for low-cost, non-critical items, but for critical equipment it causes unplanned downtime and risk.

Key takeaway: Choose run-to-failure deliberately, not by default.

Day 131 · TPM Integration

TPM Case

An illustrative TPM journey on one filler.

A TPM case study shows how a team applied autonomous and planned maintenance to one machine. This example is illustrative; use measured results from your own site.

Key takeaway: Start on one machine, prove it, then expand.

Day 132 · TPM Integration

TPM Misalignment & TPM Leadership

TPM fails when it is seen as a maintenance-only programme.

Misalignment shows when production and maintenance blame each other, or when TPM activities are not linked to OEE goals. TPM leadership sets clear targets, supports operator ownership and reviews progress at the gemba.

Key takeaway: Equipment care is a shared job.

Day 133 · TPM Integration

Autonomous Maintenance Steps: Cleaning, Inspection & Lubrication

Operators clean, inspect and lubricate their own machines.

Autonomous maintenance starts with initial cleaning to find abnormalities, then sets inspection standards and lubrication routines that operators follow daily. Problems are tagged and fixed, and the machine is kept at its basic condition.

Key takeaway: Cleaning is inspection.

Day 134 · 8D Problem Solving

8D: D1–D4 (Team, Problem, Containment, Root Cause)

Form the team, define the problem and contain it.

D1 forms a cross-functional team. D2 describes the problem with facts. D3 puts containment actions in place to protect the customer. D4 finds and verifies the root cause and the escape point.

Key takeaway: Protect the customer first, then find the cause.

Day 135 · 8D Problem Solving

8D: D5–D8 (Corrective Action, Validation, Prevention, Closure)

Fix the cause and stop it coming back.

D5 chooses permanent corrective actions. D6 implements and validates them. D7 prevents recurrence by updating systems, standards and similar processes. D8 closes the report and recognizes the team.

Key takeaway: Close the loop and share the learning.

Day 136 · 8D Problem Solving

8D Case Study

A customer reports leaking bottles. The team contains stock, traces the cause to cap torque drift and a worn chuck, replaces the chuck, adds a torque check and applies the same check on other lines. Complaints stop.

Key takeaway: Containment, cause, correction, prevention.

Day 137 · 8D Problem Solving

Weak Root Cause & 8D Culture

A weak root cause brings the problem back.

Root causes such as "operator error" or "human mistake" do not explain why the system allowed the error. A strong 8D culture asks why until the system cause is found and treats every complaint as a chance to learn.

Key takeaway: Blame the process, not the person.

Day 138 · 8D Problem Solving

NCR & CAPA

Record nonconformance and act on its cause.

A nonconformance report (NCR) records what was wrong, where and how much. Corrective and preventive action (CAPA) removes the cause of the problem and prevents similar problems, with verification of effectiveness.

Key takeaway: Record it, fix the cause, verify the fix.

Day 139 · Policy Deployment

Hoshin Kanri

Align everyone to a few breakthrough goals.

Hoshin Kanri is a strategy deployment method that selects a few breakthrough objectives, translates them into goals and actions at each level, and reviews progress regularly.

Key takeaway: Few priorities, deployed to every level.

Day 140 · Policy Deployment

KPI Alignment & Cascade Goals

Every KPI should link to a company goal.

KPI alignment connects plant measures, such as OEE and right-first-time, to business goals like cost, delivery and quality. Cascading breaks top goals into targets for each department and team so everyone knows their part.

Key takeaway: Everyone should see how their number links up.

Day 141 · Policy Deployment

Review System

Regular reviews keep plans on track.

A review system checks progress at fixed intervals: daily at team level, weekly at department level and monthly at plant level. Each review looks at results, gaps and actions, not just reports.

Key takeaway: What gets reviewed gets done.

Day 142 · Policy Deployment

Policy Deployment Case

A plant sets a goal to cut complaints by half. It cascades targets to quality, production and stores, runs focused projects and reviews monthly. Complaints fall steadily because every team works on the same priority.

Key takeaway: Focus everyone on a few goals.

Day 143 · Policy Deployment

Strategy Disconnect & Strategic Execution

Strategy fails when it is not turned into daily work.

A strategy disconnect happens when goals stay in presentations and teams work on unrelated priorities. Strategic execution assigns owners, sets milestones, provides resources and reviews progress regularly.

Key takeaway: Strategy is only as good as its execution.

Day 144 · Policy Deployment

SQDCP & Leading vs Lagging Indicators

Measure safety, quality, delivery, cost and people.

SQDCP is a simple board structure for daily management. Lagging indicators, such as injuries and complaints, show past results. Leading indicators, such as near misses reported and audits done, show whether you are doing the things that drive results.

Key takeaway: Track the drivers, not only the results.

Day 145 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

What Is DOE?

Change several factors at once and learn faster.

Design of Experiments (DOE) is a planned way to test how several input factors affect an output. Instead of changing one thing at a time, DOE changes factors together in a structured pattern, which shows main effects and interactions with fewer trials.

Key takeaway: Plan the experiment before you run it.

Day 146 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

Factors & Levels

Choose what to change and by how much.

Factors are the inputs you change, such as temperature, mixing time or speed. Levels are the settings you test, for example low and high. Choose factors from process knowledge and levels wide enough to show an effect but safe to run.

Key takeaway: Good factors and levels make a useful experiment.

Day 147 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

Full vs Fractional Design

Test every combination or a smart subset.

A full factorial design tests all combinations of factor levels and shows every effect. A fractional factorial tests a planned subset, which saves trials when there are many factors but mixes some effects together. Use fractional designs to screen, full designs to confirm.

Key takeaway: Screen with fractions, confirm with full designs.

Day 148 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

Interaction Effect

Sometimes one factor's effect depends on another.

An interaction occurs when the effect of one factor changes with the level of another. For example, a longer mixing time may help only at a higher temperature. One-factor-at-a-time testing misses interactions; DOE finds them.

Key takeaway: Factors can work together or against each other.

Day 149 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

DOE Case Study

A team tests mixing speed, time and temperature in eight trials to reduce viscosity variation. Results show temperature and its interaction with time matter most. New settings cut variation and are confirmed in production batches.

Key takeaway: Few planned trials, clear answers.

Day 150 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

DOE Misinterpretation & Data Based Decision Leadership

Statistically significant does not always mean important.

DOE results are misread when trials are not randomized, noise is ignored or significant effects are too small to matter. Data-based leaders ask for confirmation runs and judge results by practical as well as statistical significance.

Key takeaway: Confirm the result before you change the standard.
