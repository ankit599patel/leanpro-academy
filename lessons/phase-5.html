Free daily lessons, days 121–150.

Day 121 · Quality Tools

What Is Quality?

Meeting customer requirements, every time.

Quality means meeting customer and product requirements consistently. It is built into the process through good design, standards and control, not added by inspection at the end.

Key takeaway: Build quality in; do not inspect it in.

Day 122 · Quality Tools

Cost of Poor Quality

The hidden cost of getting it wrong.

Cost of poor quality (COPQ) is the cost of not doing things right first time: internal failures (scrap, rework), external failures (complaints, returns), and appraisal and prevention costs.

Key takeaway: Most quality costs are invisible until you list them.

Day 123 · Quality Tools

7 QC Tools

Seven simple tools that solve most problems.

The 7 basic quality tools are check sheet, histogram, Pareto chart, cause-and-effect diagram, scatter diagram, control chart and stratification (some lists include the flowchart). They help collect, display and analyze data.

Key takeaway: Simple tools, used well, solve most problems.

Day 124 · Quality Tools

Check Sheet

Collect data simply, at the source.

A check sheet is a simple form for recording data as it happens, usually with tally marks by category, time or location. It makes patterns visible and data easy to summarize.

Key takeaway: Easy to fill means more reliable data.

Day 125 · Quality Tools

Histogram

See the shape of your data.

A histogram is a bar chart showing how often values fall into ranges. It reveals the centre, spread and shape of the data, and whether it fits within specification limits.

Key takeaway: Look at the shape before the average.

Day 126 · Quality Tools

Control Chart

A time-ordered view with limits from the process.

A control chart shows data in time order with a centre line and upper and lower control limits calculated from the process. It separates common-cause variation from special causes that need action.

Key takeaway: Control limits come from the process, not the spec.

Day 127 · Quality Tools

Scatter Diagram

Is there a relationship between two variables?

A scatter diagram plots pairs of values to show whether two variables are related, and how strongly. A pattern suggests correlation, which still needs to be confirmed as cause.

Key takeaway: Correlation is a clue, not proof of cause.

Day 128 · Quality Tools

Stratification

Split the data to reveal hidden patterns.

Stratification separates data into groups, such as by shift, machine, operator, material lot or product, to see whether a problem is concentrated in one group.

Key takeaway: Split the data before drawing conclusions.

Day 129 · Quality Tools

Run Chart

Watch performance change over time.

A run chart plots data in time order, often with a median line. It shows trends, shifts and cycles, and is a simple first step before a control chart.

Key takeaway: Plot it over time before judging a change.

Day 130 · Quality Tools

Pareto Chart

Rank problems to focus on the vital few.

A Pareto chart shows categories as bars in descending order with a cumulative percentage line. It highlights the few categories that account for most of the problem.

Key takeaway: Start with the tallest bar.

Day 131 · Quality Tools

Process Flowchart

Draw the process to understand it.

A process flowchart shows the steps, decisions and flow of a process using standard symbols. It helps teams agree how the process actually works and spot unnecessary steps or loops.

Key takeaway: Map the process as it really is, not as it should be.

Day 132 · Quality Tools

Inspection vs Prevention

Find defects late, or stop them from happening.

Inspection detects defects after they are made. Prevention stops defects from being made, through robust process design, standards, error-proofing and control at the source.

Key takeaway: Prevention costs less than detection.

Day 133 · Quality Tools

First Pass Yield

Good units on the first try.

First pass yield (FPY) is the percentage of units that complete a process step correctly the first time, without rework, repair or re-inspection.

Key takeaway: Rework hides in yields that ignore the first pass.

Day 134 · Quality Tools

Right First Time

Do it right the first time.

Right first time (RFT) means completing a task or product correctly on the first attempt, with no corrections. It applies to products, documents, batch records and changeovers.

Key takeaway: Getting it right once is faster than fixing it twice.

Day 135 · Quality Tools

Defect Classification

Not all defects are equally serious.

Defect classification groups defects by severity, commonly critical (safety or legal risk), major (affects function or customer use) and minor (cosmetic). It guides sampling, reaction and priority.

Key takeaway: Match the reaction to the severity.

Day 136 · Quality Tools

Sampling Basics

Check a part to judge the whole.

Sampling means inspecting a selected portion of a lot to make a decision about the whole lot. Good sampling is random, representative and follows a defined plan for sample size and acceptance.

Key takeaway: A sample is only useful if it is random and planned.

Day 137 · Quality Tools

Acceptance Criteria

Know exactly what 'good' looks like.

Acceptance criteria are the defined conditions a product, material or task must meet to be accepted, such as specification limits, visual standards and test results.

Key takeaway: If 'good' is not defined, it cannot be checked.

Day 138 · Quality Tools

Quality at Source

Each step owns the quality of its output.

Quality at source means each person and process step checks and guarantees the quality of its own work before passing it on, rather than relying on inspection downstream.

Key takeaway: Do not pass a defect to the next step.

Day 139 · Quality Tools

Poka-Yoke

Make mistakes impossible, or instantly visible.

Poka-yoke (error-proofing) is a design feature or device that prevents a mistake from happening or makes it immediately obvious, so a defect is not produced or passed on.

Key takeaway: Design the process so it is hard to get wrong.

Day 140 · Quality Tools

Error-Proofing Types

Prevent, detect, or warn.

Error-proofing devices are grouped by function: control (prevent the error or stop the process) and warning (signal the error). They can work by contact, by fixed value (count) or by motion step (sequence).

Key takeaway: Prefer control devices over warnings where possible.

Day 141 · Quality Tools

Layered Process Audit

Short, frequent checks by every level of leadership.

A layered process audit (LPA) is a series of short audits of key process steps, done frequently by people at different levels, from supervisors to managers, to confirm standards are followed.

Key takeaway: Many short checks beat one long audit.

Day 142 · Quality Tools

Quality Alert

Tell everyone about a quality risk, fast.

A quality alert is a short, visual notice that communicates a specific quality issue, what to look for and what to do, to everyone affected, while the problem is being solved.

Key takeaway: Clear, visual and posted where the work happens.

Day 143 · Quality Tools

Nonconformance Report

Record what did not meet requirements.

A nonconformance report (NCR) documents a product, material or process that does not meet requirements: what was found, where, how much, immediate actions and disposition decisions.

Key takeaway: Record it clearly so it can be fixed properly.

Day 144 · Quality Tools

CAPA

Corrective and preventive action.

CAPA is a structured system to investigate problems, find root causes, implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence and preventive actions to avoid similar problems, and check effectiveness.

Key takeaway: Close a CAPA only after effectiveness is verified.

Day 145 · Quality Tools

8D Overview

Eight disciplines for solving serious problems.

8D is a team-based method for solving problems, often customer complaints: D1 team, D2 describe, D3 contain, D4 root cause, D5 choose actions, D6 implement and verify, D7 prevent recurrence, D8 recognize the team.

Key takeaway: Contain fast, solve properly, prevent recurrence.

Day 146 · Quality Tools

8D D1-D3

Team, problem description and containment.

In 8D, D1 forms a cross-functional team, D2 describes the problem with facts (what, where, when, how many), and D3 puts interim containment in place to protect the customer.

Key takeaway: Protect the customer before searching for the cause.

Day 147 · Quality Tools

8D D4-D5

Find the root cause and choose actions.

In 8D, D4 identifies and verifies the root cause of the problem and of why it was not detected. D5 selects permanent corrective actions and confirms they will address the cause.

Key takeaway: Find why it happened and why it escaped.

Day 148 · Quality Tools

8D D6-D8

Implement, prevent recurrence, recognize.

In 8D, D6 implements the permanent actions and verifies their effectiveness, D7 prevents recurrence by updating systems and similar processes, and D8 closes the report and recognizes the team.

Key takeaway: Fix it, prevent it everywhere, thank the team.

Day 149 · Quality Tools

Quality Case Study

An illustrative quality story on mislabelling.

A quality case study connects the problem, containment, root cause, countermeasure and verification. This example is illustrative; use your own data when presenting a real case.

Key takeaway: Contain, find the cause, error-proof, verify.

Day 150 · Quality Tools

Quality Review

Prevent, measure, analyze and control quality.

This month covered quality tools: define what good looks like, collect data with simple tools, find the vital few causes, prevent defects at the source and solve serious problems with CAPA and 8D.

Key takeaway: Measure, find the cause, prevent, control.
