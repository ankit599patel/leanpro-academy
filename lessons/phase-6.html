Day 151 · TPM & Maintenance

What Is TPM?

Everyone takes care of the equipment.

Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) is a system for maximizing equipment effectiveness by involving everyone, from operators to managers, in preventing breakdowns, defects and accidents.

Key takeaway: Equipment care is everyone's job.

Day 152 · TPM & Maintenance

TPM Pillars

The pillars that hold up TPM.

TPM is often described with pillars: autonomous maintenance, planned maintenance, focused improvement, quality maintenance, early equipment management, training, safety and environment, and TPM in offices, built on a 5S foundation.

Key takeaway: Build on 5S, then raise the pillars.

Day 153 · TPM & Maintenance

OEE Overview

How much of the planned time is truly productive.

Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) measures how well equipment is used during planned production time. OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality.

Key takeaway: OEE shows where the losses are, not who to blame.

Day 154 · TPM & Maintenance

Availability

Was the machine running when it should be?

Availability is the share of planned production time that the equipment actually runs. It is reduced by downtime such as breakdowns, changeovers and waiting for material.

Key takeaway: Every unplanned stop reduces availability.

Day 155 · TPM & Maintenance

Performance

Did it run as fast as it should?

Performance compares actual output with what the equipment could produce at its ideal cycle time during run time. It is reduced by slow running and minor stops.

Key takeaway: Small stops and slow speed hide here.

Day 156 · TPM & Maintenance

Quality Rate

How much of the output was good?

The quality rate in OEE is good units divided by total units produced. It is reduced by scrap, rework and start-up rejects.

Key takeaway: Count rework as a loss, not as good output.

Day 157 · TPM & Maintenance

OEE Calculation

Put the three factors together.

OEE is calculated by multiplying availability, performance and quality. Each factor is a percentage of the ideal, so OEE shows overall effectiveness in one number.

Key takeaway: Improve the factor with the biggest loss first.

Day 158 · TPM & Maintenance

Six Big Losses

The losses that reduce equipment effectiveness.

The six big losses are breakdowns, setup and adjustments (availability losses), minor stops and reduced speed (performance losses), and start-up rejects and production rejects (quality losses).

Key takeaway: Name the loss before trying to fix it.

Day 159 · TPM & Maintenance

Autonomous Maintenance

Operators care for their own equipment.

Autonomous maintenance gives operators responsibility for basic equipment care: cleaning, inspection, lubrication, tightening and early detection of abnormalities, following clear standards.

Key takeaway: Operators are the first line of equipment care.

Day 160 · TPM & Maintenance

Initial Cleaning

Clean to find hidden problems.

Initial cleaning is the first step of autonomous maintenance: a thorough cleaning of the equipment that restores it to basic condition and reveals hidden defects such as leaks, cracks and loose parts.

Key takeaway: Every tag found during cleaning is a future breakdown avoided.

Day 161 · TPM & Maintenance

Inspection Standards

Define what to check, how and how often.

Inspection standards list each check point on the equipment, the method, the normal condition, the frequency and who does it, often with photos, so checks are consistent.

Key takeaway: Make normal visible so abnormal is obvious.

Day 162 · TPM & Maintenance

Lubrication Basics

The right lubricant, amount and time.

Lubrication basics cover using the correct lubricant, in the correct amount, at the correct point and interval, and keeping lubricants clean and clearly labelled.

Key takeaway: Right lubricant, right point, right amount, right time.

Day 163 · TPM & Maintenance

Planned Maintenance

Maintenance scheduled before failure.

Planned maintenance is maintenance work scheduled in advance, based on time, usage or condition, to prevent failures and keep equipment in good condition.

Key takeaway: Plan the stop before the machine plans it for you.

Day 164 · TPM & Maintenance

Preventive vs Predictive

Fixed intervals versus actual condition.

Preventive maintenance is done at fixed intervals of time or usage. Predictive maintenance uses condition data, such as vibration, temperature or oil analysis, to decide when maintenance is needed.

Key takeaway: Use condition data where failures give warning signs.

Day 165 · TPM & Maintenance

Breakdown Maintenance

Run to failure, then repair.

Breakdown maintenance means repairing equipment after it fails. It can be acceptable for low-cost, non-critical items, but for critical equipment it causes unplanned downtime and risk.

Key takeaway: Choose run-to-failure deliberately, not by default.

Day 166 · TPM & Maintenance

MTBF

Average time between failures.

Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) is the average running time between one failure and the next. A higher MTBF means more reliable equipment.

Key takeaway: Track failures to see reliability improve.

Day 167 · TPM & Maintenance

MTTR

Average time to repair a failure.

Mean Time To Repair (MTTR) is the average time needed to restore equipment after a failure, including diagnosis, waiting for parts and repair. A lower MTTR means faster recovery.

Key takeaway: Spares, skills and standards cut repair time.

Day 168 · TPM & Maintenance

Maintenance Backlog

Work waiting to be done.

Maintenance backlog is the amount of approved maintenance work not yet completed, often expressed in hours or weeks of available labour. A growing backlog signals that capacity or planning needs attention.

Key takeaway: Watch the backlog trend, not just the size.

Day 169 · TPM & Maintenance

Criticality Analysis

Focus effort on the equipment that matters most.

Criticality analysis ranks equipment by the impact of its failure on safety, quality, delivery and cost, and by how likely failure is, to decide maintenance priorities and strategies.

Key takeaway: Not all equipment deserves the same strategy.

Day 170 · TPM & Maintenance

Spare Parts Strategy

The right spares, in the right quantity.

A spare parts strategy decides which parts to stock, how many and where, based on equipment criticality, lead time, failure rate and cost, balancing availability against inventory.

Key takeaway: Stock for criticality and lead time, not for comfort.

Day 171 · TPM & Maintenance

Failure Mode Basics

How a part fails, and what happens next.

A failure mode is the way a component or process can fail, such as wear, leakage, cracking or misalignment. Understanding failure modes, causes and effects helps plan prevention.

Key takeaway: Know how it fails to know how to prevent it.

Day 172 · TPM & Maintenance

FMEA

Find and rank risks before they become failures.

Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) systematically lists possible failure modes, their effects and causes, rates severity, occurrence and detection, and prioritizes actions to reduce the highest risks.

Key takeaway: Act on the highest risks before they occur.

Day 173 · TPM & Maintenance

Condition Monitoring

Listen to the machine before it fails.

Condition monitoring tracks indicators of equipment health, such as vibration, temperature, noise, oil condition or current, to detect developing problems early.

Key takeaway: Trends warn you before breakdowns do.

Day 174 · TPM & Maintenance

Vibration Basics

Vibration tells the story of rotating equipment.

Vibration analysis measures the vibration of rotating equipment to detect problems such as imbalance, misalignment, looseness and bearing wear, often before they can be heard or felt.

Key takeaway: Set a baseline, then watch the trend.

Day 175 · TPM & Maintenance

Thermography

See heat that signals trouble.

Thermography uses infrared cameras to show temperature differences on equipment. Hot spots can reveal loose electrical connections, overloaded circuits, failing bearings or insulation problems.

Key takeaway: Hot spots are early warnings.

Day 176 · TPM & Maintenance

Maintenance Planning

Prepare the job before the technician starts.

Maintenance planning defines what work is needed, how to do it safely, which parts, tools, skills and permits are required, and how long it will take, before the job is scheduled.

Key takeaway: A well-planned job is a faster, safer job.

Day 177 · TPM & Maintenance

Maintenance Scheduling

Put planned jobs on the calendar.

Maintenance scheduling assigns planned jobs to specific dates, times and people, coordinated with the production plan so equipment is available and work is done efficiently.

Key takeaway: Schedule with production, not against it.

Day 178 · TPM & Maintenance

TPM Case Study

An illustrative TPM journey on one filler.

A TPM case study shows how a team applied autonomous and planned maintenance to one machine. This example is illustrative; use measured results from your own site.

Key takeaway: Start on one machine, prove it, then expand.

Day 179 · TPM & Maintenance

OEE Improvement Example

Use OEE to find and fix the biggest loss.

An OEE improvement starts by breaking OEE into its factors and losses, picking the largest loss, finding its causes and fixing them, then tracking OEE to confirm the gain.

Key takeaway: Break it down, pick the biggest loss, fix it.

Day 180 · TPM & Maintenance

TPM Review

Care, measure and improve equipment together.

This month covered TPM: involve operators in daily care, plan maintenance before failure, measure OEE and its losses, use reliability measures like MTBF and MTTR, and focus on critical equipment.

Key takeaway: Care daily, plan ahead, measure losses, improve.
