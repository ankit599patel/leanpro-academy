DOE, Risk & ISO Systems

Free daily lessons, days 151–180.

Day 151 · Design of Experiments (DOE)

Sampling Basics

Check a part to judge the whole.

Sampling means inspecting a selected portion of a lot to make a decision about the whole lot. Good sampling is random, representative and follows a defined plan for sample size and acceptance.

Key takeaway: A sample is only useful if it is random and planned.

Day 152 · Risk Based Thinking

What is Risk?

Risk is the chance that something affects your objectives.

Risk combines how likely an event is and how serious its effect would be. Risk-based thinking means identifying what could go wrong, or right, before it happens and acting in proportion to the risk.

Key takeaway: Think ahead, act in proportion.

Day 153 · Risk Based Thinking

FMEA Structure & RPN Calculation

Find and rank risks before they become failures.

Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) systematically lists possible failure modes, their effects and causes, rates severity, occurrence and detection, and prioritizes actions to reduce the highest risks.

Key takeaway: Act on the highest risks before they occur.

Day 154 · Risk Based Thinking

Risk Mitigation Planning

Reduce high risks with clear actions and owners.

For each high risk, choose an action: avoid, reduce, transfer or accept. Give each action an owner and a date, then reassess the risk after the action to confirm it has fallen.

Key takeaway: Every high risk needs an owner and a date.

Day 155 · Risk Based Thinking

Risk Case Example

An FMEA on a filling line ranks a missing seal as high severity. The team adds an automatic check and an alarm. Detection improves, the RPN falls sharply and the defect no longer reaches customers.

Key takeaway: Rank the risk, then design it out.

Day 156 · Risk Based Thinking

Ignoring Preventive Actions & Proactive Leadership

Preventive action is cheaper than recovery.

Organisations often ignore preventive actions until a failure happens. Proactive leaders review risks regularly, fund prevention and reward people who raise risks early.

Key takeaway: Reward the people who spot risks early.

Day 157 · Risk Based Thinking

Criticality Analysis

Focus effort on the equipment that matters most.

Criticality analysis ranks equipment by the impact of its failure on safety, quality, delivery and cost, and by how likely failure is, to decide maintenance priorities and strategies.

Key takeaway: Not all equipment deserves the same strategy.

Day 158 · ISO 9001 Integration

Process Approach

Manage activities as connected processes.

The process approach in ISO 9001 defines each process with its inputs, outputs, owner, resources and measures, and shows how processes link. Managing the links prevents gaps between departments.

Key takeaway: Manage the connections, not just the parts.

Day 159 · ISO 9001 Integration

Clause Structure

ISO 9001 follows a common high-level structure.

Clauses 4 to 10 cover context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement. This structure follows Plan-Do-Check-Act and is shared with other ISO standards, which makes integration easier.

Key takeaway: The clauses follow PDCA.

Day 160 · ISO 9001 Integration

Risk & Opportunity

Plan for what could go wrong and what could go well.

ISO 9001 asks organisations to identify risks and opportunities that affect product quality and customer satisfaction, plan actions and check whether they worked.

Key takeaway: Risks and opportunities both need action.

Day 161 · ISO 9001 Integration

Document Control

The right version, in the right place, at the right time.

Document control ensures documents are approved before use, reviewed and updated, clearly identified by version and available where needed. Obsolete documents must be removed or marked to prevent use.

Key takeaway: Old documents cause new mistakes.

Day 162 · ISO 9001 Integration

Quality Audit Case

An internal audit finds that operators use an old SOP printout. The root cause is no control of printed copies. The plant introduces controlled copies with version numbers and a monthly check, closing the gap.

Key takeaway: Audits find system gaps, not culprits.

Day 163 · ISO 9001 Integration

Compliance Only Mindset & Quality System Leadership

Certificates on the wall do not make quality.

A compliance-only mindset treats ISO as paperwork for the auditor. Quality system leadership uses the system to run the business: clear processes, real data and continual improvement.

Key takeaway: Use the system to improve, not just to pass audits.

Day 164 · ISO 9001 Integration

Batch Traceability & Lot Control

Trace every batch from raw material to customer.

Batch traceability links each finished batch to the raw material lots, equipment, people and records used to make it. Lot control keeps lots identified and separated. In a complaint or recall, it shows exactly which product is affected.

Key takeaway: If you cannot trace it, you cannot contain it.

Day 165 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Environmental Aspects

Identify how your activities affect the environment.

Environmental aspects are parts of your activities that interact with the environment, such as emissions, effluent, waste, energy use and spills. Each aspect can cause an impact on air, water, soil or people.

Key takeaway: List the aspects before you judge the impacts.

Day 166 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Impact Evaluation

Rank environmental impacts by significance.

Evaluate each aspect by severity, likelihood, scale and legal requirements to decide which are significant. Significant aspects get objectives, controls and monitoring.

Key takeaway: Focus effort on significant aspects.

Day 167 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Legal Compliance

Know and meet every environmental requirement.

Legal compliance means identifying applicable laws, consents and permits, such as effluent limits and hazardous waste rules, and checking compliance regularly. Keep evidence and act quickly on any gap.

Key takeaway: Compliance is the minimum, not the goal.

Day 168 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Waste Management Strategy

Reduce, reuse, recycle, then dispose safely.

A waste management strategy follows the waste hierarchy: prevent waste first, then reuse, recycle and recover, and dispose safely only what remains. Segregate waste at source and track quantities by type.

Key takeaway: The best waste is the waste never made.

Day 169 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Environmental Improvement Case

A plant measures solvent losses and finds leaks at transfer points. Closed transfer and better seals reduce losses and emissions, saving material cost as well as improving compliance.

Key takeaway: Environmental waste is also money waste.

Day 170 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Reactive Environmental System & Sustainability Leadership

Do not wait for a complaint or a notice.

A reactive environmental system responds only after incidents or inspections. Sustainability leadership sets targets, monitors key aspects and builds environmental care into daily work.

Key takeaway: Prevent, do not react.

Day 171 · ISO 14001 (Environmental)

Chemical Compatibility & SDS Essentials

Store chemicals safely and know their hazards.

Chemical compatibility means keeping substances that react dangerously, such as oxidizers and flammables, apart. The Safety Data Sheet (SDS) gives hazards, handling, storage, PPE and first aid for each chemical and must be available where it is used.

Key takeaway: Read the SDS before you handle the chemical.

Day 172 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Hazard Identification

Something that can harm, versus how likely and how bad.

A hazard is anything with the potential to cause harm, such as a chemical, a moving part or a height. Risk combines how likely harm is to happen and how severe it would be.

Key takeaway: Control the hazard to lower the risk.

Day 173 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Risk Assessment Matrix

Identify, evaluate and control risks before work.

A risk assessment identifies hazards in a task, evaluates the risk, decides on controls, records the result and reviews it when the task or conditions change.

Key takeaway: Assess before you act; review when things change.

Day 174 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Control Hierarchy

Start with the most effective control.

The hierarchy of controls ranks risk controls from most to least effective: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and personal protective equipment (PPE).

Key takeaway: PPE is the last line of defence, not the first.

Day 175 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Incident Investigation

Learn from what happened to stop it happening again.

Incident investigation gathers facts about an event, establishes the sequence, identifies immediate and underlying causes, and agrees actions to prevent recurrence, without blaming individuals.

Key takeaway: Investigate to learn, not to blame.

Day 176 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Safety Improvement Case

A plant analyses near misses and finds many forklift and pedestrian conflicts. Marked walkways, mirrors and one-way routes are introduced. Near misses in the area fall and no injury occurs.

Key takeaway: Near misses point to the next accident.

Day 177 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Paper Safety System & Safety Driven Culture

How people think and act about safety when no one is watching.

Safety culture is the shared values, beliefs and behaviours about safety in an organization. A strong culture shows in open reporting, leaders who listen, and everyone feeling able to stop unsafe work.

Key takeaway: Anyone can stop unsafe work, and is thanked for it.

Day 178 · ISO 45001 (Safety)

Permit to Work & LOTO

Control high-risk work before it starts.

A permit to work authorizes specific high-risk jobs, such as hot work or confined space entry, after hazards are checked and controls are in place. Lockout/tagout (LOTO) isolates energy sources and locks them so equipment cannot start during maintenance.

Key takeaway: No permit, no work. No lock, no work.

Day 179 · Integrated Management System (IMS)

What is IMS?

One system for quality, environment and safety.

An Integrated Management System (IMS) combines standards such as ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 into one system with shared processes, documents, audits and reviews.

Key takeaway: One system, many standards.

Day 180 · Integrated Management System (IMS)

System Integration

Combine what is common, keep what is specific.

System integration merges common elements such as policy, document control, training, audits, management review and corrective action, while keeping specific controls for each standard.

Key takeaway: Integrate the common elements first.
