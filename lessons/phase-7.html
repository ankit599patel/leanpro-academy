Day 181 · Standard Work & Productivity

What Is Standard Work?

The current best way to do the job, written down.

Standard work documents the current best and safest method for a task, with three elements: the work sequence, the time to do it (against takt) and the standard work-in-process. It is the baseline for improvement.

Key takeaway: No standard, no improvement.

Day 182 · Standard Work & Productivity

Takt Time

The pace the customer demands.

Takt time is the rate at which products must be completed to meet customer demand. Takt time = available production time ÷ customer demand for that period.

Key takeaway: Takt is set by demand, not by the machine.

Day 183 · Standard Work & Productivity

Cycle Time

How long one cycle of the work takes.

Cycle time is the time it takes to complete one cycle of an operation or produce one unit at a process step, measured from start to start or end to end.

Key takeaway: Measure cycle time at the process, not from the spreadsheet.

Day 184 · Standard Work & Productivity

Lead Time

The total time from start to finish.

Lead time is the total elapsed time from a request or order to delivery, or from material arrival to product shipment. It includes processing, waiting, inspection, movement and storage.

Key takeaway: Customers feel lead time, not cycle time.

Day 185 · Standard Work & Productivity

Work Sequence

The order in which the work is done.

The work sequence is the defined order of steps an operator follows to complete a task safely and efficiently. A consistent sequence reduces variation in time and quality.

Key takeaway: Same sequence, every time, by everyone.

Day 186 · Standard Work & Productivity

Standard WIP

The minimum work-in-process for smooth flow.

Standard WIP is the minimum amount of material that must be within a process, including units in machines, to keep the work flowing according to the standard sequence.

Key takeaway: Too much WIP hides problems; too little stops flow.

Day 187 · Standard Work & Productivity

Line Balancing

Share the work evenly against takt.

Line balancing distributes work across stations so that each station's cycle time is close to, but not above, takt time. It reduces waiting at some stations and overload at others.

Key takeaway: Balance the work to the pace of demand.

Day 188 · Standard Work & Productivity

Yamazumi Chart

A stacked chart of work content per operator.

A Yamazumi chart shows each operator's or station's work as stacked blocks of tasks, compared with a takt line. It makes imbalance and non-value-added work easy to see.

Key takeaway: See the imbalance, then rebalance the tasks.

Day 189 · Standard Work & Productivity

Bottleneck

The step that limits the whole process.

A bottleneck is the process step with the lowest capacity relative to demand. It sets the output of the whole system; improving other steps does not increase output.

Key takeaway: An hour lost at the bottleneck is an hour lost for the whole line.

Day 190 · Standard Work & Productivity

Theory of Constraints

Manage the system through its constraint.

The Theory of Constraints (TOC) says every system has at least one constraint that limits its output. Its five focusing steps are: identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, and repeat.

Key takeaway: Improve the constraint first; everything else follows.

Day 191 · Standard Work & Productivity

Constraint Management

Keep the constraint running, always.

Constraint management means protecting the constraint's time: keep a buffer in front of it, prevent its stoppages, check quality before it, and run it through breaks and changeovers where possible.

Key takeaway: Protect every minute of the constraint.

Day 192 · Standard Work & Productivity

Capacity vs Demand

Can we make what customers want?

Capacity is the maximum output a process can produce in a period. Comparing capacity with demand shows whether the plant can meet orders, has spare capacity or must add shifts, improve OEE or invest.

Key takeaway: Know your real capacity, not the nameplate.

Day 193 · Standard Work & Productivity

Productivity vs Utilization

Busy is not the same as productive.

Productivity is useful output per unit of input. Utilization is how much of the available time a resource is busy. High utilization of a non-constraint can create excess inventory without more sales.

Key takeaway: Measure output that customers need, not activity.

Day 194 · Standard Work & Productivity

Man-Machine Chart

See how people and machines share time.

A man-machine chart shows, on a timeline, when the operator and the machine are working, waiting or idle. It helps find waiting time and better ways to combine tasks.

Key takeaway: Use machine time to do useful operator work.

Day 195 · Standard Work & Productivity

Spaghetti Diagram

Draw the walking paths to see wasted motion.

A spaghetti diagram traces the actual path of a person or material through the work area on a layout drawing. Long, crossing paths reveal motion and transport waste.

Key takeaway: If the lines look like spaghetti, the layout needs work.

Day 196 · Standard Work & Productivity

Work Combination Table

Combine manual work, machine time and walking.

A standard work combination table shows each step's manual time, machine time and walking time on a timeline against takt, so the work can be arranged to fit within takt.

Key takeaway: Fit manual, machine and walking time inside takt.

Day 197 · Standard Work & Productivity

Time Study

Measure how long the work really takes.

A time study observes and times a task over several cycles, breaks it into elements, and records the repeatable time for each element, along with any irregular events.

Key takeaway: Observe several cycles; record what really happens.

Day 198 · Standard Work & Productivity

Motion Economy

Make every movement easier and shorter.

Principles of motion economy aim to reduce effort and time: keep items within easy reach, use both hands, avoid unnecessary movements, use gravity feeds and design comfortable work heights.

Key takeaway: Short, easy, natural movements.

Day 199 · Standard Work & Productivity

Ergonomics Basics

Fit the work to the person.

Ergonomics designs tasks, workstations and tools to fit people's capabilities, reducing strain from lifting, awkward postures, repetition and force.

Key takeaway: Good ergonomics is good productivity.

Day 200 · Standard Work & Productivity

SMED Overview

Single-Minute Exchange of Die: faster changeovers.

SMED is a method for reducing changeover time by separating internal setup (done while stopped) from external setup (done while running), converting internal to external, and streamlining both.

Key takeaway: Do as much as possible while the machine is still running.

Day 201 · Standard Work & Productivity

Internal vs External Setup

What must happen while stopped, and what need not.

Internal setup tasks can only be done while the machine is stopped, such as changing a nozzle. External setup tasks can be done while it is running, such as fetching parts, tools and documents.

Key takeaway: Move every possible task outside the stop.

Day 202 · Standard Work & Productivity

Setup Reduction

Shorten every step of the changeover.

Setup reduction streamlines changeovers by using quick-release fasteners, standard heights and positions, pre-set tools, parallel work by two people and eliminating adjustments.

Key takeaway: Eliminate adjustments; set it right first time.

Day 203 · Standard Work & Productivity

Changeover Checklist

Everything ready before the line stops.

A changeover checklist lists the parts, tools, materials, documents and people needed, and the steps in order, so nothing is missing when the changeover starts.

Key takeaway: Check before you stop, not after.

Day 204 · Standard Work & Productivity

Production Pitch

A regular interval to check output against plan.

Pitch is a set time interval, often a multiple of takt that matches a standard pack quantity, used to release work and check whether production is on schedule throughout the shift.

Key takeaway: Check progress at every pitch, not just at shift end.

Day 205 · Standard Work & Productivity

Daily Production Control

Know every hour if you are on plan.

Daily production control tracks planned versus actual output at regular intervals during the shift, records reasons for gaps and triggers quick responses, usually on an hour-by-hour board.

Key takeaway: React during the shift, not the next day.

Day 206 · Standard Work & Productivity

Standard Work Audit

Check that the standard is followed and still right.

A standard work audit observes whether the work is done according to the standard and whether the standard is still the best method. Gaps lead to coaching or to updating the standard.

Key takeaway: Audit to learn, then update or coach.

An illustrative productivity improvement.

A productivity case study shows how a team improved good output per resource used. This example is illustrative; use measured data from your own process.

Key takeaway: Balance, simplify, track, then verify.

Day 208 · Standard Work & Productivity

Bottleneck Case Study

An illustrative bottleneck story.

A bottleneck case study shows how finding and protecting the constraint increased system output. This example is illustrative; use your own data in a real case.

Key takeaway: Find the constraint, protect it, then improve it.

Day 209 · Standard Work & Productivity

Standard Work Review

Sequence, time and WIP, against takt.

Standard work review: document the best current method with a clear sequence, cycle time compared with takt and standard WIP; audit it regularly; and update it when an improvement is proven.

Key takeaway: The standard is the base; improvement builds on it.

Day 210 · Standard Work & Productivity

Productivity Review

Productivity tools from takt to SMED.

Productivity review: match work to takt, balance the line, find and protect the bottleneck, reduce changeovers with SMED, remove motion waste and track output during the shift.

Key takeaway: Pace, balance, constraint, changeover, control.
