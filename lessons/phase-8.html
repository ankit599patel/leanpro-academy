Excellence, Kaizen & Finance

Free daily lessons, days 211–240.

Day 211 · ESG & Sustainability

ESG Improvement Case

A plant sets targets for energy and water per tonne, installs meters and runs Kaizen on utilities. Consumption per tonne falls and the data supports customer sustainability audits.

Key takeaway: Lean tools improve ESG results too.

Day 212 · ESG & Sustainability

Greenwashing Mistake & Sustainable Leadership

Claims must match real performance.

Greenwashing means making environmental claims that are not supported by data. Sustainable leaders set honest targets, report real progress and invest in genuine improvement.

Key takeaway: Report facts, not slogans.

Day 213 · ESG & Sustainability

Safety Observation & Behavior-Based Safety

Observe behaviour and coach for safety.

Safety observation means watching work, noting safe and unsafe acts and discussing them with the worker. Behaviour-based safety uses these observations to reinforce safe habits and remove the causes of unsafe acts.

Key takeaway: Coach behaviour; fix conditions.

Day 214 · Business Excellence Models

What is Business Excellence?

Excellence is consistent performance across the whole business.

Business excellence means achieving outstanding results for customers, people, society and owners through strong leadership, processes and continual improvement. Excellence models give a framework to assess and improve.

Key takeaway: Excellence is built, not declared.

Day 215 · Business Excellence Models

EFQM Overview

A European model for organisational excellence.

The EFQM Model looks at direction (purpose, strategy, culture), execution (stakeholder engagement, value creation, performance) and results. It helps organisations assess maturity and plan improvement.

Key takeaway: Assess, then improve.

Day 216 · Business Excellence Models

Baldrige Concept

A framework based on criteria for performance excellence.

The Baldrige framework assesses leadership, strategy, customers, measurement, workforce, operations and results. It focuses on how processes are designed, deployed, learned from and integrated.

Key takeaway: Strong processes create strong results.

Day 217 · Business Excellence Models

KPI Alignment

Link every KPI to the strategy.

In excellence models, KPIs are chosen to show progress on strategic goals, with clear targets, owners and review cycles. Too many KPIs dilute focus.

Key takeaway: Fewer, linked KPIs.

Day 218 · Business Excellence Models

Business Excellence Case

A plant uses an excellence self-assessment to find gaps in leadership review and customer feedback. Two focused projects close the gaps, and the next assessment shows higher maturity.

Key takeaway: Self-assessment shows where to start.

Day 219 · Business Excellence Models

Overcomplicated Systems & Enterprise Vision

Keep the system simple enough to use.

Overcomplicated systems create paperwork and confusion. An enterprise vision gives one clear direction so everyone understands priorities and how their work contributes.

Key takeaway: Simple systems, clear vision.

Day 220 · Business Excellence Models

Lean Excellence Self-Assessment

See where you are today.

A Lean excellence self-assessment rates your area against key practices, such as 5S, standard work, visual management, problem solving, TPM and daily management, to find strengths and gaps.

Key takeaway: Score honestly, with evidence, then pick two gaps.

Day 221 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Kaizen vs Innovation

Small steady steps versus big leaps.

Kaizen improves through many small, low-cost steps by everyone. Innovation makes larger jumps through new technology or investment. Organizations need both, and kaizen keeps gains from slipping between leaps.

Key takeaway: Use innovation to leap; use kaizen to climb and hold.

Day 222 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Cross Functional Kaizen

Solve problems that cross department boundaries.

Cross-functional Kaizen brings people from production, maintenance, quality and stores together to solve problems no single department can fix alone, such as long changeovers or material delays.

Key takeaway: Big problems need mixed teams.

Day 223 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Kaizen Funnel

Collect many ideas, select the best, implement fast.

A Kaizen funnel captures ideas widely, screens them for value and effort and moves the best into action. Tracking ideas through the funnel shows where they get stuck.

Key takeaway: Many ideas in, the best ones out.

Day 224 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Impact Measurement

Measure the activity and the results of kaizen.

Kaizen metrics track both effort (such as ideas submitted, implemented and people involved) and outcomes (such as safety, quality, delivery and cost improvements linked to the changes).

Key takeaway: Measure participation and results together.

Day 225 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Kaizen Case

An illustrative kaizen at a labelling station.

A kaizen case study shows the problem, the small changes tried and how the result was verified. This example is illustrative; replace it with measured results from your own site.

Key takeaway: Show the before, the change and the verified after.

Day 226 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

Idea Without Execution & Continuous Improvement Leadership

Ideas without action destroy trust.

When ideas are collected but not implemented, people stop suggesting. Continuous improvement leaders respond fast, support implementation and celebrate results.

Key takeaway: Act on ideas quickly.

Day 227 · Advanced Kaizen Strategy

PDCA / PDSA Cycle

Plan, Do, Check, Act: the basic improvement cycle.

PDCA plans a change, tries it on a small scale, checks the results against the plan and acts by standardizing or adjusting. PDSA replaces Check with Study to stress learning from the results.

Key takeaway: Small cycles, fast learning.

Day 228 · Financial Impact of Lean

Cost Structure

Know where the money goes.

Cost structure shows how total cost splits into materials, labour, energy, overheads and other costs, fixed and variable. Lean focuses improvement on the largest controllable costs.

Key takeaway: Improve where the cost really is.

Day 229 · Financial Impact of Lean

Working Capital

Cash tied in stock and receivables cannot be used.

Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities. Lean reduces it by lowering inventory and lead time, which frees cash for the business.

Key takeaway: Less inventory means more cash.

Day 230 · Financial Impact of Lean

Productivity vs Profit

More output only helps if it is sold.

Productivity measures output per input. It turns into profit only when the extra output meets real demand or the freed resources reduce cost. Otherwise it creates inventory.

Key takeaway: Link productivity to demand.

Day 231 · Financial Impact of Lean

ROI Calculation

Show the return on improvement.

Return on investment is net benefit divided by cost, usually per year. Count only verified savings, such as reduced scrap, overtime or material, and include all costs of the change.

Key takeaway: Prove the money, not just the idea.

Day 232 · Financial Impact of Lean

Financial Impact Case

A scrap reduction project saves material and rework hours. Finance validates the savings, and the result is shown in the monthly cost report, building trust in the improvement programme.

Key takeaway: Validated savings build credibility.

Day 233 · Financial Impact of Lean

Cost Cutting vs Lean & Business Thinking Leader

Cost cutting removes resources; Lean removes waste.

Cutting costs without removing waste often harms quality and morale. Business-thinking leaders use Lean to remove waste so costs fall while capability grows.

Key takeaway: Remove waste, not capability.

Day 234 · Financial Impact of Lean

Verify Results

Prove the improvement with before/after facts.

Verifying results means comparing measured performance before and after a change, over a long enough period, to confirm the improvement is real and the problem has not moved elsewhere.

Key takeaway: No data, no claim.

Day 235 · Benchmarking

Internal Benchmark

Compare lines, shifts and plants inside the company.

Internal benchmarking compares performance between similar areas, such as two filling lines. It is quick and the data is accessible, and best practices can be shared directly.

Key takeaway: Learn from your best line first.

Day 236 · Benchmarking

External Benchmark

Learn from the best outside your company.

External benchmarking compares performance with other companies or industries. It shows what is possible and brings new ideas, but must compare like with like.

Key takeaway: Look outside to see what is possible.

Day 237 · Benchmarking

KPI Comparison

Compare KPIs using the same definitions.

KPI comparison only works when definitions, units and periods are the same. For example, OEE must use the same planned time and ideal cycle definitions in every area compared.

Key takeaway: Same definitions, fair comparison.

Day 238 · Benchmarking

Gap Analysis

Measure the distance to the target.

Gap analysis compares current performance with the target or benchmark and identifies the causes of the gap. It turns benchmarking into an action plan.

Key takeaway: Know the gap, then close it.

Day 239 · Benchmarking

Benchmarking Case

Two lines making the same product have very different changeover times. The faster line's method is studied, standardized and applied to the slower line, cutting its changeover time.

Key takeaway: Best practice travels inside the company.

Day 240 · Benchmarking

Copy Paste Mistake & Competitive Strategy

Adapt best practice; do not just copy it.

Copying another plant's solution without understanding its context often fails. A competitive strategy learns principles from benchmarks and adapts them to your own process and people.

Key takeaway: Understand why it works before you copy.
