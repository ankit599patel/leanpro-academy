Day 211 · VSM & Flow

What Is Value Stream Mapping?

See the whole flow of material and information.

Value stream mapping (VSM) is a visual method for drawing all the steps, material flows and information flows needed to deliver a product, with key data, to find waste and plan a better future state.

Key takeaway: Map the whole stream before improving any part.

Day 212 · VSM & Flow

Current State Map

Draw the stream as it really is today.

A current state map shows how the value stream works now, based on direct observation: process steps, data boxes, inventory, information flows and a timeline of lead time versus processing time.

Key takeaway: Walk it and draw it as it is, not as it should be.

Day 213 · VSM & Flow

Future State Map

Design how the stream should flow.

A future state map shows how the value stream should work after improvement, using Lean principles: produce to takt, create flow where possible, use pull where flow is not possible, and schedule at one point.

Key takeaway: Design the future, then plan the steps to get there.

Day 214 · VSM & Flow

Information Flow

How each step knows what to do next.

Information flow shows how orders, forecasts and schedules move from the customer through planning to each process. Complicated or multiple schedules often cause overproduction and confusion.

Key takeaway: Simplify the information, and the material will follow.

Day 215 · VSM & Flow

Material Flow

How material moves between steps.

Material flow shows how materials move through the value stream: pushed to the next step, flowing continuously, or pulled from a supermarket. It also shows where inventory accumulates.

Key takeaway: Where material waits, lead time grows.

Day 216 · VSM & Flow

Process Data Box

The key facts for each process step.

A process data box records key data under each step on a value stream map, such as cycle time, changeover time, uptime, number of operators, batch size and shifts.

Key takeaway: Collect data at the process, not from old reports.

Day 217 · VSM & Flow

Timeline Calculation

Add up waiting and processing along the stream.

The VSM timeline shows, step by step, the waiting time (often estimated from inventory ÷ daily demand) and the processing time. Totals give lead time and processing time.

Key takeaway: Inventory divided by demand gives waiting time.

Day 218 · VSM & Flow

VA vs NVA Time

How much of the time actually adds value?

On a value stream, value-added (VA) time is the time when the product is transformed in a way the customer pays for. Non-value-added (NVA) time is everything else. The VA share is often very small.

Key takeaway: Shrink the waiting, not just the work.

Day 219 · VSM & Flow

Value Stream Lead Time

From raw material to the customer's hands.

Value stream lead time is the total time a product takes to move through the whole stream, including all waiting and storage. It is the time the customer and the business actually experience.

Key takeaway: Lower inventory between steps to cut lead time.

Day 220 · VSM & Flow

Takt-Based Flow

Set the pace of the stream by demand.

Takt-based flow means designing the value stream so the pacemaker process runs at takt time and other steps support that pace, avoiding overproduction and starvation.

Key takeaway: One pace for the whole stream: takt.

Day 221 · VSM & Flow

Continuous Flow

Move product without stopping between steps.

Continuous flow means producing and moving one item, or a small batch, at a time through a series of steps, with each step doing only what the next one needs.

Key takeaway: Flow where you can; pull where you cannot.

Day 222 · VSM & Flow

One-Piece Flow

Make and move one piece at a time.

One-piece flow means each unit moves to the next step as soon as it is complete. It shortens lead time, exposes defects immediately and reduces WIP, but needs balanced, reliable steps.

Key takeaway: Smaller batches, faster feedback.

Day 223 · VSM & Flow

Batch Size

Smaller batches, shorter lead time.

Batch size is the number of units processed together before moving on. Large batches increase waiting and inventory; reducing batch size usually needs shorter changeovers.

Key takeaway: Reduce changeover time to reduce batch size.

Day 224 · VSM & Flow

FIFO Lane

First in, first out, with a limit.

A FIFO lane is a controlled queue between two processes that holds a maximum quantity in sequence. When it is full, the upstream process stops, preventing overproduction.

Key takeaway: Fixed order, fixed limit.

Day 225 · VSM & Flow

Supermarket Pull

Replenish only what was taken.

A supermarket is a controlled store of parts or products between processes. The downstream process takes what it needs, and the upstream process replenishes only what was taken.

Key takeaway: Take what you need; replace what was taken.

Day 226 · VSM & Flow

Kanban Basics

A signal to produce or move.

Kanban is a signalling system, using cards, bins or electronic signals, that authorizes production or movement of a set quantity when the downstream process uses material.

Key takeaway: No signal, no production.

Day 227 · VSM & Flow

Kanban Card

What a kanban card must tell you.

A kanban card carries the information needed to act: item name and code, quantity, supplier process or location, customer process or location, and card number.

Key takeaway: Clear cards prevent wrong refills.

Day 228 · VSM & Flow

WIP Limits

Cap the work in progress to keep it flowing.

A WIP limit sets the maximum number of items allowed in a process step or queue. When the limit is reached, no new work starts until something finishes, which exposes bottlenecks.

Key takeaway: Stop starting; start finishing.

Day 229 · VSM & Flow

Heijunka

Level the mix and volume of production.

Heijunka is production levelling: spreading the volume and mix of products evenly over time instead of producing large batches of one product, so the upstream flow is steady.

Key takeaway: Level the schedule to steady the flow.

Day 230 · VSM & Flow

Production Leveling

Even out the daily load.

Production levelling smooths the total volume produced each day or shift, so people, machines and suppliers see a steady workload rather than peaks and troughs.

Key takeaway: Steady load, steady quality.

Day 231 · VSM & Flow

Pacemaker Process

The one step that sets the pace.

The pacemaker process is the single point in the value stream that receives the schedule. Steps upstream are replenished by pull; steps downstream follow in flow.

Key takeaway: Schedule one point, not every point.

Day 232 · VSM & Flow

Milk Run

A fixed route that delivers regularly.

A milk run is a fixed route and schedule for delivering materials to several points or collecting from several suppliers, in small, frequent quantities.

Key takeaway: Fixed route, fixed time, small loads.

Day 233 · VSM & Flow

Layout Optimization

Arrange the space to suit the flow.

Layout optimization arranges equipment, storage and workstations so materials and people travel the shortest, safest path in the order of the process, with clear aisles and access.

Key takeaway: Let the process sequence shape the layout.

Day 234 · VSM & Flow

Cellular Manufacturing

Group steps into a compact cell.

Cellular manufacturing groups the machines and people needed for a product family into a compact cell, often U-shaped, so work flows step to step with minimal transport and WIP.

Key takeaway: Bring the steps together; let the product flow.

Day 235 · VSM & Flow

Flow Interruptions

What stops the flow, and how to find it.

Flow interruptions are events that stop or slow product movement: breakdowns, missing material, quality holds, changeovers, waiting for approval and imbalance between steps.

Key takeaway: Log every interruption; fix the most frequent.

Day 236 · VSM & Flow

VSM Common Mistakes

Traps that make maps less useful.

Common VSM mistakes include mapping from the office, mapping too many products at once, skipping information flow, using old data and creating a future state without an action plan.

Key takeaway: Map from the floor, then act on the map.

Day 237 · VSM & Flow

VSM Case Study

An illustrative value stream improvement.

A VSM case study shows the current state, the future state design and the actions taken. This example is illustrative; use your own measured data for real cases.

Key takeaway: Current state, future state, action plan, review.

Day 238 · VSM & Flow

Future State Actions

Turn the future state into a plan.

Future state actions are the specific projects, owners and dates needed to move from the current to the future state, often grouped into loops and reviewed regularly.

Key takeaway: A future state without owners is just a drawing.

Day 239 · VSM & Flow

Flow Metrics

Measure how well work flows.

Flow metrics show how smoothly work moves: lead time, WIP, throughput, VA ratio and on-time delivery. Tracked together, they show whether flow improvements are working.

Key takeaway: Track lead time and WIP together.

Day 240 · VSM & Flow

VSM Review

See the stream, design the flow, pull the rest.

VSM and flow review: map the current state from the floor, design a future state with takt, flow, pull and levelling, create an action plan with owners, and measure lead time and WIP.

Key takeaway: Map, design, act, measure, repeat.
