Free daily lessons, days 241–270.

Day 241 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety as a Lean Foundation

No improvement is worth an injury.

Safety is the foundation of Lean: a stable, organized and standardized workplace is also a safer one. Every improvement should be checked for its effect on safety before it is adopted.

Key takeaway: Safety first in every change.

Day 242 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Near Miss Reporting

Report the event that almost happened.

A near miss is an unplanned event that could have caused injury, illness or damage but did not. Reporting near misses lets teams fix hazards before someone is hurt.

Key takeaway: Every near miss is a free lesson.

Day 243 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Hazard vs Risk

Something that can harm, versus how likely and how bad.

A hazard is anything with the potential to cause harm, such as a chemical, a moving part or a height. Risk combines how likely harm is to happen and how severe it would be.

Key takeaway: Control the hazard to lower the risk.

Day 244 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Risk Assessment

Identify, evaluate and control risks before work.

A risk assessment identifies hazards in a task, evaluates the risk, decides on controls, records the result and reviews it when the task or conditions change.

Key takeaway: Assess before you act; review when things change.

Day 245 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Hierarchy of Controls

Start with the most effective control.

The hierarchy of controls ranks risk controls from most to least effective: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and personal protective equipment (PPE).

Key takeaway: PPE is the last line of defence, not the first.

Day 246 · Safety & EHS Excellence

5S for Safety

An organized workplace is a safer workplace.

5S supports safety by removing trip hazards, keeping exits and emergency equipment clear, marking hazardous zones, and making unsafe conditions easy to see.

Key takeaway: Clutter hides hazards; order reveals them.

Day 247 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Unsafe Condition vs Act

Fix the condition and understand the behaviour.

An unsafe condition is a hazard in the workplace, such as a missing guard or a wet floor. An unsafe act is a behaviour that increases risk, such as bypassing a guard. Most incidents involve both.

Key takeaway: Ask why the unsafe act made sense to the person.

Day 248 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Permit to Work Basics

Authorize high-risk work in a controlled way.

A permit-to-work system controls high-risk, non-routine tasks, such as hot work, confined space entry or work at height, by defining the hazards, controls, isolations and authorizations before work starts.

Key takeaway: No permit, no high-risk work.

Day 249 · Safety & EHS Excellence

LOTO Overview

Lock out, tag out, verify zero energy.

Lockout/tagout (LOTO) is a procedure to isolate all energy sources, such as electrical, mechanical, pneumatic and hydraulic, apply personal locks and tags, and verify zero energy before maintenance.

Key takeaway: Try to start it before you touch it.

Day 250 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Machine Guarding

Keep people away from moving parts.

Machine guarding prevents contact with dangerous moving parts using fixed guards, interlocked guards, light curtains and other devices. Guards must not be easy to bypass.

Key takeaway: If a guard is bypassed, ask why and fix that.

Day 251 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Chemical Compatibility

Some chemicals must never meet.

Chemical compatibility means knowing which substances can react dangerously when mixed or stored together, such as acids with bases or oxidizers with flammables, and segregating them accordingly.

Key takeaway: Check the SDS before you store or mix.

Day 252 · Safety & EHS Excellence

SDS Essentials

The first place to look for chemical hazards.

A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) provides key information on a chemical: hazards, composition, first aid, firefighting, handling and storage, exposure controls, PPE and disposal, in a standard section format.

Key takeaway: Read the SDS before you handle it.

Day 253 · Safety & EHS Excellence

PPE Selection

The right protection for the specific hazard.

PPE selection starts from the risk assessment and the SDS: choose equipment that protects against the specific hazard, fits the user, is compatible with other PPE, and is maintained and worn correctly.

Key takeaway: Match the PPE to the hazard, not to habit.

Day 254 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Emergency Preparedness

Know what to do before it happens.

Emergency preparedness means planning and practising the response to events such as fire, chemical spills, injuries and power failures: roles, alarms, routes, assembly points, equipment and drills.

Key takeaway: Practise the plan before you need it.

Day 255 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Fire Safety Basics

Remove one side of the fire triangle.

Fire needs fuel, heat (an ignition source) and oxygen. Fire safety controls these by managing flammable materials, controlling ignition sources, and providing detection, suppression and escape.

Key takeaway: Control fuel and ignition before a fire starts.

Day 256 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Ergonomics & Safety

Prevent strain injuries by design.

Ergonomics in safety reduces musculoskeletal injuries by limiting heavy lifting, repetitive movements, awkward postures and forceful exertions through workplace and task design.

Key takeaway: Design out the strain, do not just train it out.

Day 257 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Observation

Watch, talk and learn about safe work.

A safety observation is a short, structured observation of a task, followed by a respectful conversation about what was done safely and what could be safer.

Key takeaway: Observe to learn, not to catch people out.

Day 258 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Behavior-Based Safety

Understand and improve everyday safety behaviour.

Behaviour-based safety focuses on observing, discussing and reinforcing safe behaviours, while also addressing the systems and conditions that influence those behaviours.

Key takeaway: Fix the system that shapes the behaviour.

Day 259 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Kaizen

Small improvements that remove hazards.

A safety kaizen is a small, quick improvement that reduces a specific hazard, made by the people doing the work, often after a near miss or observation.

Key takeaway: See a hazard, fix it small and fast.

Day 260 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Incident Investigation

Learn from what happened to stop it happening again.

Incident investigation gathers facts about an event, establishes the sequence, identifies immediate and underlying causes, and agrees actions to prevent recurrence, without blaming individuals.

Key takeaway: Investigate to learn, not to blame.

Day 261 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Root Cause of Incidents

Look beyond the person to the system.

Root cause analysis of incidents asks why the conditions and behaviours existed, looking at design, procedures, training, supervision and culture, so actions address underlying causes.

Key takeaway: 'Human error' is where the investigation starts.

Day 262 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety KPI Basics

Measure safety ahead of and after the event.

Safety KPIs include lagging indicators, such as injury rates, which measure outcomes, and leading indicators, such as near-miss reports, observations and actions closed, which measure prevention activity.

Key takeaway: Lead with prevention measures; learn from outcome measures.

Day 263 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Walk

Go and see safety where the work is done.

A safety walk is a planned visit by leaders to the workplace to observe conditions and work practices, talk with employees about risks, and follow up on actions.

Key takeaway: Ask, listen and follow up.

Day 264 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Contractor Safety

Same standards for everyone on site.

Contractor safety ensures that contractors are selected for competence, briefed on site hazards and rules, work under the right permits, are supervised, and are included in incident reporting.

Key takeaway: Contractors get the same protection as employees.

Day 265 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Training Matrix

Who is trained in what, at a glance.

A safety training matrix lists each person against the safety training they need, such as LOTO, forklift, chemical handling and first aid, showing status and refresher due dates.

Key takeaway: No training, no task.

Day 266 · Safety & EHS Excellence

EHS Visual Management

Make safety status visible to everyone.

EHS visual management displays safety and environmental information at the workplace: hazard signs, PPE zones, emergency equipment locations, safety cross calendars and open actions.

Key takeaway: If safety is visible, it is talked about.

Day 267 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Case Study

An illustrative safety improvement story.

A safety case study shows the hazard, how it was found, the controls chosen using the hierarchy of controls, and how effectiveness was checked. This example is illustrative.

Key takeaway: Use the hierarchy, then verify the control works.

Day 268 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Risk Reduction Prioritization

Tackle the biggest risks first.

Risk reduction prioritization ranks risks by severity and likelihood, then applies the most effective practical controls first to the highest risks, while keeping track of the rest.

Key takeaway: Highest risk first, strongest control first.

Day 269 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Review

Hazards, controls, people and culture.

Safety review: identify hazards, assess risk, apply the hierarchy of controls, control energy and chemicals, prepare for emergencies, learn from near misses and incidents, and lead visibly.

Key takeaway: Identify, control, learn, lead.

Day 270 · Safety & EHS Excellence

Safety Culture

How people think and act about safety when no one is watching.

Safety culture is the shared values, beliefs and behaviours about safety in an organization. A strong culture shows in open reporting, leaders who listen, and everyone feeling able to stop unsafe work.

Key takeaway: Anyone can stop unsafe work, and is thanked for it.
